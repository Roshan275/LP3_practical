# Assignment 16 — Create a wallet and perform test crypto transactions

## Aim

Create a MetaMask wallet and demonstrate how an account receives and sends a cryptocurrency transaction. Complete the demonstration using **Sepolia test ETH**, not real cryptocurrency. This is a wallet/software practical and does not require program code.

## What you need

- MetaMask from the official [download page](https://metamask.io/download/).
- A new practice wallet. Use an existing real-money wallet only if your instructor specifically directs you to; a separate practice wallet is safer for learning.
- Sepolia test ETH from an official faucet. Test ETH has no real-world value.
- A second account you control for the send/receive demonstration.

## Procedure

1. Install MetaMask and select **Create a new wallet**. Follow the current setup prompts. MetaMask's current setup may offer a Secret Recovery Phrase or an account sign-in method; follow the official [wallet creation guide](https://support.metamask.io/start/creating-a-new-wallet/).
2. If using a Secret Recovery Phrase, store it offline in a private place. Do not take a screenshot of it, upload it, or include it in your practical journal. MetaMask warns never to share it; anyone who has it can control the wallet.
3. In MetaMask, enable test networks and choose **Sepolia**. Follow [MetaMask's current testnet steps](https://support.metamask.io/configure/networks/how-to-view-testnets-in-metamask/).
4. Copy your account's public address. Request a small amount of Sepolia test ETH using the official [MetaMask faucet guidance](https://support.metamask.io/develop/blockchain-networks/testnets/using-the-testnet-faucet/). Do not pay anyone for test ETH.
5. Demonstrate receiving: have a second account you control send a small amount of Sepolia ETH to this account's public address. If you only have one account, create a second practice account in MetaMask and send between them.
6. Wait for the incoming transaction to confirm. Record the sending and receiving account labels (you may abbreviate addresses in your journal), amount, transaction hash, and status.
7. Demonstrate sending: from the funded account, send a small amount back to the second account. Review the recipient, network, amount, and fee before confirming.
8. Open transaction details and record gas used and transaction fee. Use the transaction's block-explorer link to verify its status if available.
9. Summarize the roles of the public address (receive identifier), private key/Secret Recovery Phrase (controls access), blockchain (records confirmed transactions), and gas fee (pays for transaction processing).

## Practical journal checklist

- Aim and required software.
- Steps followed to create the wallet (do **not** record any secret words or keys).
- Network used: Sepolia testnet.
- One incoming and one outgoing transaction: status, amount, abbreviated hash, and fee.
- Screenshots of the MetaMask account and transaction details, with any private information hidden.
- Short conclusion explaining the receive/send process and transaction fees.

## Expected result

You can show one confirmed incoming and one confirmed outgoing testnet transaction, and explain that a wallet account is controlled by its credentials while transactions are recorded on the selected network. Sepolia ETH is not real money.

## Safety checklist

- Never share a Secret Recovery Phrase or private key with anyone, including support staff or your instructor.
- Only use official MetaMask pages; ignore unsolicited messages offering wallet help or free coins.
- Confirm the wallet is on **Sepolia** before requesting or sending test ETH.
- Do not connect your practice wallet to unknown sites or approve unknown requests.

## Suggested conclusion

“I created a MetaMask practice wallet, received and sent Sepolia test ETH, and verified both transactions. I observed that the wallet address is used for receiving, while secret credentials must remain private. Testnet transactions are for learning and have no real-world value.”
