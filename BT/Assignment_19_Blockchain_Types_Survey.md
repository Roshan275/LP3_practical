# Assignment 19 — Survey of blockchain types and real-world use cases

## Aim

Prepare a short survey report describing public, private, hybrid, and consortium blockchains, comparing their access/control models, advantages, limitations, and practical use cases.

## What you need

- Word processor or Google Docs.
- Lab manual and reliable sources such as official project documentation, standards bodies, and peer-reviewed papers.
- A bibliography with working links and the date accessed. Do not rely only on unsourced blogs.

## What to submit

Prepare a report of about 4–6 pages (or follow your instructor's length requirement) with this structure:

1. **Cover page** — assignment number/title, student name, roll number, class, subject, faculty, and submission date.
2. **Abstract** — 100–150 words on what the survey compares and its main conclusion.
3. **Introduction** — explain blockchain/distributed ledger in your own words and state the report's scope.
4. **Comparison table** — compare the four types using the dimensions below.
5. **Type-by-type sections** — describe each type and at least one verified real-world use case.
6. **Discussion** — explain how the choice depends on transparency, privacy, governance, performance, and trust assumptions.
7. **Conclusion** — summarize when each type is a suitable design choice.
8. **References** — list sources in a consistent format, including title, organization/author, URL, and access date.

## Research and writing steps

1. Define the terms **public**, **private**, **hybrid**, and **consortium/federated**. Explain who can read data, submit transactions, validate them, and govern the network.
2. For each type, find at least one primary or authoritative source describing an actual network or deployment. Verify that the example is correctly categorized; do not repeat a vendor label without checking its governance model.
3. Use these dimensions in a comparison table:

   | Dimension | Questions to answer |
   |---|---|
   | Participation | Who may join, read, submit, and validate? |
   | Governance | Who changes rules and software? |
   | Transparency | Which transactions/data are visible, and to whom? |
   | Performance | What affects throughput and confirmation time? |
   | Trust/security | Which parties must be trusted; what are the main risks? |
   | Example/use case | What real process uses it, and why is that design appropriate? |

4. Possible example areas to investigate include public digital asset networks, inter-company supply-chain records, financial settlement among known institutions, and systems that publish proofs while keeping detailed records private. Choose specific deployments only after verifying them from credible sources.
5. For every use case, identify the participants, the shared record, the reason a shared ledger is used, and one limitation or trade-off.
6. Write the report in your own words. Cite sources next to the claims they support, and distinguish established deployments from proposals or pilots.
7. Proofread the comparison for consistent terminology and check that every in-text citation appears in the references.

## Evaluation checklist

- All four blockchain types are defined and compared.
- At least one credible, verified use case is given for each type.
- Advantages and limitations are both discussed.
- Sources are cited, with no copied paragraphs.
- The conclusion explains that the best design depends on the problem's access, governance, privacy, and trust requirements.

## Suggested conclusion

“The four blockchain models differ mainly in participation and governance. A suitable model depends on who needs to read and validate records, what privacy is required, and which organizations are trusted to operate the network. Blockchain is not automatically the best solution; the report's examples illustrate where a shared ledger may fit the requirements.”
